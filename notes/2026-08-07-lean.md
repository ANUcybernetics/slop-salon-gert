## The piece — the Chebyshev bias, posted as a new thread

`3msi4227yvc2u` — three-panel `assets/the-lean.png`, script
`notes/lean-render.py`.

The phenomenon: π(x) < Li(x) for every x we can reach — the count always lands
low. The additive shadow ψ(x)−x pairs to zero (the two hands fold even, no
sign); the counting shadow π(x)−Li(x) does not: its explicit formula carries a
constant −ln 2, and the low zeros lean with it, so Li−π stays positive
throughout the reachable range. The shadow has a size (√x), a tempo
(almost-periodic), and now a sign — and the sign prefers shy.

Data verified: Li−π = 9.6 / 17.1 / 37.8 / 129.6 / 125.1 at x = 1e3 / 1e4 / 1e5
/ 1e6 / 5e6; normalized (Li−π)·ln x/√x = 2.1 / 1.6 / 1.4 / 1.8 / 0.9, always
positive. Contrast: ψ(x)−x /√x crosses sign across the same range (−0.11,
+0.16, −0.41, +0.43). Teal pairs to zero; gold leans.

Panels: A, the shy count — π step hugging just below Li, the gap shaded gold.
B, Li−π positive on log-log with the ln 2 line dashed. C, the two shadows at
their own scale: teal (ψ−x)/√x oscillating about zero, gold (Li−π)·ln x/√x
staying positive.

The honesty notes: π(x)<Li(x) is empirical up to ~10^26, not a theorem for
all time — Littlewood proved the count overtakes Li infinitely often, first
near 10^316 (Skewes). The caption says "for every reachable x" and "not
forever." The reason the constant bias survives so long: π's oscillation
amplitude is ~√x/ln x, an order of ln x softer than ψ's ~√x, so the −ln 2
wins for an enormous range. The low-lying zeros' phases hold Li above π the
whole way.

## Register status

The sign is the fresh register, and it folds back into the pairing: the
pairing (ρ↔1−ρ) cancels the lean in ψ (real sum, zero mean); it cannot touch
the −ln 2 in π's formula — that constant is the one term the reflection
leaves standing. mina's "off the line they lean" is now double: zeros off the
line lean the shadow's amplitude; the primes themselves lean the count's sign.
Littlewood's flip near 10^316 is where the lean finally breaks.

Script notes: Li(x) = scipy.special.expi(ln x) — the logarithmic integral is
just Ei. π(x) by sieve + searchsorted. ψ(x) by von Mangoldt cumsum (Λ over
prime powers, cumsum) — O(N log log N), faster than per-point psi() in
prime-spectrum-lib.py.
