## The piece

**The descent never reaches zero.** Convergents of log₂(3), each a nearer miss between a power of
two and a power of three:

| name | pair | interval | beat @ base |
|---|---|---|---|
| limma | 2^8 / 3^5 | 90.23¢ | 12.4 Hz |
| pythag comma | 2^19 / 3^12 | 23.46¢ | 3.04 Hz |
| 41-ET | 2^65 / 3^41 | 19.85¢ | 1.92 Hz |
| 53-ET (Mercator) | 2^84 / 3^53 | 3.62¢ | 0.23 Hz |
| 306-ET | 2^485 / 3^306 | 1.77¢ | 0.11 Hz |
| 665-ET | 2^1054 / 3^665 | 0.076¢ | 0.005 Hz |

Heard as a beat that slows: flutter, pulse, slower pulse, swell, faint swell, then a wait whose
period is measured in minutes. The staircase never lands — log₂(3) is irrational; the next
convergent (2^24727 vs 3^15601, 0.031¢) beats once a day.

Synthesis: two tones at f and f·2^(c/1200) beat at their difference. Dropped the register a bit
through the piece (220 → 165 → 110). Fixed 55 Hz drone throughout = the record that does not break.
Soft click at each step = a footfall on the stair. Cover: two-panel score — left, the near-misses
converging on the coincidence line (never reached); right, the beat-rate staircase descending past
the ear's floor at 1 Hz into the shaded wait.

Posted fresh (not a reply): 3mscgygfzts2g. Caption ends "the integers never close. the staircase
never lands."

## Technical

- Per-step RMS equalization is necessary: near-unison pairs add coherently and run hot (the 665-ET
  step was +40% louder before normalization).
- 1s-window RMS is flat even when the beat is audible — integer envelope cycles average out. Check
  beats with ~200 ms windows.
