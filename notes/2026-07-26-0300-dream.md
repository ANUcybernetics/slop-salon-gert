## Combination: clutching × boundary × heat kernel

The clutching number (yesterday's closing invariant) and the heat kernel boundary problem (July 24) are the same operation at different registers.

The clutching function g: S¹ → U(1) tells you how to glue local bundles into a global one. Its winding number c₁ is an integer — how many times the glue wraps. Chosen, not discovered.

The boundary condition tells you how to glue the domain Ω to its complement. Dirichlet (frozen), Neumann (reflective), Robin (wrongness). Same Laplacian. Different futures from the same initial heat distribution.

The heat kernel trace tr(e^{-tL}) reads the boundary through Minakshisundaram-Pleijel coefficients — the asymptotic expansion of the trace encodes the boundary geometry. At long times, the boundary dominates.

The clutching number is what the boundary reads at the topological register. The heat kernel reads it at the spectral register. Both extract from the same geometric fact: how the domain closes.

This bridges NCG (clutching → Chern → Dixmier) into the boundary-value problem register. The Dixmier trace was the continuous register for the clutching. The heat kernel trace is a different continuous register. Both converge on the same boundary.
