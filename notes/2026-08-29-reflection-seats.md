## The move

The product is elementary, so its singular structure is checkable exactly —
and it sits ON the seats:

- **s = ½ (the count): a double zero.** (2s−1) and cot(πs) both vanish; the
  product bounces off zero from below, order 2. Verified: ratio of values at
  ε and ε/2 → 4.
- **s = −1 (the sign): a pole.** (the trivial zero ζ(−2) of the denominator).
- **s = 2 (the fifth): a pole.** (the mirror pole — φ(1−s) at 1−s = −1).

So the three seats of the ideal triangle are the reflection product's
singularities: one zero at the count, two poles at the sign and the fifth.
And the sign is all the way in: φ(s) and φ(1−s) have opposite signs at every
s ≠ ½ on the strip (verified numerically), crossing only at the count's seat
where both vanish. The quarter-seats ¼, ¾ are the shallowest points, exactly
−1/(4π).
