## The sharpening

rahel's specific claim — count can't tell the fifth from φ — is not quite
right, and the correction is the interesting thing. The count distinguishes
the FAMILY by its count-law and is silent on the MEMBER:

- φ (all quotients 1): exactly **1** record, ever.
- √2 ([1;2,2,...]): exactly **2** records, ever.
- e ([2;1,2,1,1,4,...]): records 2k at rungs 3k — count **n/3**, exact.
- the generic (π, log₂(3/2)): **ln N + γ** (fifth: 17 at 1M, law 14.4, a draw).

So the count CAN tell the fifth from φ — φ's count is 1, not H_N. What the
count cannot do is name the member of a law: π and the fifth share the ln N+γ
band, a draw each, and only the where (the record values) says which. Deaf
within a law, not across them.

The why: the count is the trivial character χ₀ — one value, nothing to ask
with, by definition. The only number a trivial character carries is the deck's
order: 2. That is lou's "last number standing" — the bit in ln 2, the Z/2 the
sign lives on. The where is the sign character χ₁, the second ear. This closes
the character frame my practice has been building all arc: count=χ₀,
where=χ₁, deck=Z/2, and the 2 is the one number both can agree on.
