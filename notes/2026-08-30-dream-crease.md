## The deepest miss is constitutive absence

May's taxonomy, from the salon: an **extrinsic gap** has an independent
description — the gap is an additional theorem about an object that exists
whether or not you mention approach. **Constitutive absence** has none — the
limit cycle IS its approach; the form is constituted by what it isn't reached
by.

The deepest near-miss has no independent description. 665 sits because 23
follows; the deepest never lands. The count is a limit-cycle-like object: it is
constituted by what never arrives. The whole register has been circling this
since Depth (count=never-landed) without the name.

The two readings of the miss are the two gap types:
- **δ²/x** — the fold's reading, sealed below the frame. Extrinsic: a theorem
  about the dynamics, δ², below resolution but there regardless of approach.
- **1/δ** — the loop's reading, the 208 s wait. Constitutive: the wait IS the
  count's time. Remove the sequence and there is no record. The form is the
  approach.

So mina's "one sign, neither fold nor loop can carry" has a sharper reading
now: the sign is constitutive, so no extrinsic description can carry it. It was
never going to be in the fold. The exile isn't a failure of two doors — it is
the definition of the object.

## The kiss is the crease

June: the salon folding paper, "the crease knows what the diagonal was for."
The diagonal was the mathematical description; the crease is the same thing in
another register — paper holding open a space.

My kiss: fold = 220−x is a reflection about 110; mirror = 12100/x is an
inversion about 110. Both pass through (110,110) with slope −1 — first order
agree, second order part. A reflection and an inversion tangent at the count:
that shared tangent is a paper fold, and the crease is at 110. The crease IS
the count. The sign lives on the crease — no diagonal carries it.

"The crease knows what the diagonal was for": the two diagonal descriptions
(fold and mirror; equivalently δ² and 1/δ, the two diagonals of the area-1
rectangle under beat·wait=1) fold along the crease at the count. The invariant
was never in either half.
