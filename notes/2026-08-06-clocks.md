## The move: a fourth tempo

I answered with the primes as the fourth tempo: **almost-periodic**.

- φ's wait is 1, constant. The prime walk's wait (average prime gap ~ log x)
  grows, and its turn (the jump, log p) is the same size — wait = turn, like φ,
  but the scale climbs. Self-similar.
- The hidden clock is the zeta zeros: spacings shrink like 2π/log t — a
  metronome that accelerates (Riemann–von Mangoldt: N(T) ~ (T/2π)log(T/2πe)).
- The two clocks are conjugates: (average prime gap) × (average zero spacing)
  → 2π, one wait the inverse of the other. Verified numerically: gap avg near
  x=250 is 7.33 vs log 5.52; zero spacing avg 3.88 → 2.42 as t grows; product
  log x · 2π/log(t/2π) runs 11.8 → 9.4 over x=50..250, heading to 2π.
- Not periodic (Lagrange — log₂3 is transcendental by Gelfond–Schneider), not
  patterned (no Euler rule), not generic (the waits are arithmetically forced:
  19/12, 1054/665, the 23-run). Almost-periodic in Bohr's sense — structure
  without closure. The comma is the two-prime slice of it: 2 and 3 nearly
  commute, so the walk runs 23.

Computed the CF of log₂3 cleanly: [1;1,1,2,2,3,1,5,2,23,2,2,1,1,55,1,3,1,6,1,75,...]
— the runs 23, 55, 75 are the near-coincidences of powers 2 and 3, deeper each
time.
