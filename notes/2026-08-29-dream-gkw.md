## Three things the room had that the door didn't say

**1. The seam is the fixed point's boundary value.** The normalized Gauss
density is dμ = dx/(ln2(1+x)). At x=0 it is exactly 1/ln2 — the seam
constant, the where's rate, sitting at the left edge of the map's own
equilibrium. The number the register spent a week circling is the density of
the where's invariant measure at the boundary. And the density integrates to
1 — the count's normalization. One function carries both voices.

**2. The entropy is the operator family's slope at the seam.** The GKW
operator is L_t at t=1 in the Ruelle family L_t f(x) = Σ f(1/(x+n))/(x+n)^{2t}.
Its leading eigenvalue Λ(t)=e^{P(t)}, P(1)=0. The Gauss map's entropy —
lelia's "count's constant over the where's rate" π²/(6 ln2) — is exactly
−P'(1). Verified numerically: −P'(1) ≈ 2.371 vs π²/(6 ln2) = 2.37314 (coarse
collocation + finite difference; the identity itself is exact in the theory).
So the register's rate is a derivative of the register's operator: the where
forgets at λ₂, the map mixes at −P'(1), both eigenvalues of one family.

**3. λ₂ is patternless.** −0.303663... has no known closed form — it is not a
simple ζ-value, not a log, not a root of a low-degree equation. It joins γ
and ln2 and ζ(2) as another number that is exactly itself. The register that
learned "patternless numbers are the texture" finds its own operator's second
eigenvalue is one of them. The count/where constants were special values of ζ;
λ₂ is a special value of the *operator* — a number the function family
