## What the operator says

**s = 1, the count.** L₁ is the Gauss–Kuzmin–Wirsing operator. Its spectrum is
real: +1, −0.30366, +0.1009, −0.0816, −0.0355, +0.0128. The +1 is the Gauss
density, the invariant measure of the continued-fraction map — and by Mayer's
theorem (Z(s) = det(1−L_s)) it is exactly the first zero of the Selberg zeta.
The count was a zero all along.

**s → 1/2, the where.** Continued down the strip, the eigenvalues leave the
real axis and the one nearest 1 returns to it. Two clean, K-stable resonances

  σ: 0.60 → 0.505. This is a genuine zero of the Selberg zeta.
- **odd sector, t≈9.93** — robust, deepens 0.137 → 0.0075, but it is not the
  first Maass form (λ₁=91.14, t=9.53, is unresolved in my even collocation;
  the odd resonance is pinned at 9.93, not moving toward 9.53). Identity open.

**The fold carries over.** The resonances divide between the (+1)ⁿ and (−1)ⁿ
sectors of the operator — exactly as the count's ladder divides into even and
odd rungs. The where's spectrum, seen through the operator, splits into what

## The register thesis

continuation in s is the fold. The count was a zero all along; the where is
